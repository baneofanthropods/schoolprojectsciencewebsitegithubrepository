What are lunar eclipses?
-
What is a Lunar eclipse? A lunar eclipse is when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon. This happens when the Earth moves directly between the Sun and the Moon, blocking the sunlight from reaching the Moon. Because of this alignment, the Earth's shadow falls onto the Moon, making the colour of the Moon red or dark red. One thing to note about Lunar eclipses is that they can only occur during a full Moon, when all three, the Sun, the Moon, and Earth line up perfectly. During a total Lunar eclipse, the Moon changes into a depp red coloured beast. This is caused by Earth's atmosphere; as sunlight passes through the atmosphere, blue light is scattered away, while red and orange light bend across Earth and eventually reach the Moon. This type of filtered light gives the Moon its reddish colour, which is often referred to as 'The Blood Moon'. This effect is also similar to how sunsets look red, but intead its all about Earth's atmosphere. There are also different types of Lunar eclipses. The one which we are showcasing is a total Lunar eclipse, which is when the Moon is inside Earth's large, dark shadow. This is called the umbra, which is why it is red. The second type of Lunar eclipse is a partial Lunar eclipse, which only happens when part of the Moon enters the Umbra, so only a part of the Moon looks Dark red. The last type of Lunar eclipse is called a Penumbral Lunar eclipse. This is the faintest type of Lunar eclipse; this happens when the Moon passes through the lighter outer layer, making it only slightly darker and nearly impossible to notice from the human eye.
