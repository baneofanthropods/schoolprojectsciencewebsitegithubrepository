What is a lunar eclipse?
-
What is a Lunar eclipse? A lunar eclipse is when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon. This happens when the Earth moves directly between the Sun and the Moon, blocking the sunlight from reaching the Moon. Because of this alignment, the Earth's shadow falls onto the Moon, making the colour of the Moon red or dark red. One thing to note about Lunar eclipses is that they can only occur during a full Moon, when all three, the Sun, the Moon, and Earth line up perfectly. During a total Lunar eclipse, the Moon changes into a depp red coloured beast. This is caused by Earth's atmosphere; as sunlight passes through the atmosphere, blue light is scattered away, while red and orange light bend across Earth and eventually reach the Moon. This type of filtered light gives the Moon its reddish colour, which is often referred to as 'The Blood Moon'. This effect is also similar to how sunsets look red, but intead its all about Earth's atmosphere. There are also different types of Lunar eclipses. The one which we are showcasing is a total Lunar eclipse, which is when the Moon is inside Earth's large, dark shadow. This is called the umbra, which is why it is red. The second type of Lunar eclipse is a partial Lunar eclipse, which only happens when part of the Moon enters the Umbra, so only a part of the Moon looks Dark red. The last type of Lunar eclipse is called a Penumbral Lunar eclipse. This is the faintest type of Lunar eclipse; this happens when the Moon passes through the lighter outer layer, making it only slightly darker and nearly impossible to notice with the human eye.

What is a solar eclipse?
- 
What is a solar eclipse? A solar eclipse is when the Moves in its revolution around Earth and lines up perfectly between the Sun and the Earth. Because of this alignment, the Moon blocks sunlight and casts a shadow on a small part of Earth's surface. This shadow then falls on different hemispheres depending on where Earth is in its rotation on its axis. A solar eclipse is a type of eclipse that is only possible to occur during the New Moon phase, when the Moon is positioned directly between the Sun and the Earth. This allows the extraordinary solar eclipse to occur. During a solar eclipse, people satnding on the darkest part of the Moons shadow can see a Total solar eclipse, where the Sun is completely covered and looks like a ring covered in fire. The people who are standing in lighter shadows see a partial eclipse, where only a part of the Sun is blocked. Solar eclipses vastly influence tides because when the Sun, Earth, and the Moon line up, their combined gravitational pull creates stronger spring tides. Spring tides just mean tides that produce higher high tides and lower low tides. You may be wondering, Why don't solar eclipses occur every month? This is because the Moon's orbit is tilted, so its shadow usually passes over or below Earth. Solar eclipses can only occur during special times of the year, which are called 'eclipse seasons'. In this season, the alignment of the Earth, Moon, and Sun becomes perfect enough for an eclipse to occur. Eclipse seasons only happen about 2 times a year, meaning solar eclipses can only happen about twice a year as well. This makes them extremely rare, as they can be in pretty much the entire world from Greenland to Australia, and they only happen twice a year. In simpler terms, a solar eclipse is when the Moon covers the Sun and creates a flaming golden ring around itself (the Moon), which makes an unbelievably cool effect to witness from Earth.
