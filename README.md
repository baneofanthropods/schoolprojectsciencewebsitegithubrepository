What is a lunar eclipse?
-
What is a Lunar eclipse? A lunar eclipse is when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon. This happens when the Earth moves directly between the Sun and the Moon, blocking the sunlight from reaching the Moon. Because of this alignment, the Earth's shadow falls onto the Moon, making the colour of the Moon red or dark red. One thing to note about Lunar eclipses is that they can only occur during a full Moon, when all three, the Sun, the Moon, and Earth line up perfectly. During a total Lunar eclipse, the Moon changes into a deep red coloured beast. This is caused by Earth's atmosphere; as sunlight passes through the atmosphere, blue light is scattered away, while red and orange light bend across Earth and eventually reach the Moon. This type of filtered light gives the Moon its reddish colour, which is often referred to as 'The Blood Moon'. This effect is also similar to how sunsets look red, but intead its all about Earth's atmosphere. There are also different types of Lunar eclipses. The one which we are showcasing is a total Lunar eclipse, which is when the Moon is inside Earth's large, dark shadow. This is called the umbra, which is why it is red. The second type of Lunar eclipse is a partial Lunar eclipse, which only happens when part of the Moon enters the Umbra, so only a part of the Moon looks Dark red. The last type of Lunar eclipse is called a Penumbral Lunar eclipse. This is the faintest type of Lunar eclipse; this happens when the Moon passes through the lighter outer layer, making it only slightly darker and nearly impossible to notice with the human eye.

What is a solar eclipse?
- 
What is a solar eclipse? A solar eclipse is when the Moves in its revolution around Earth and lines up perfectly between the Sun and the Earth. Because of this alignment, the Moon blocks sunlight and casts a shadow on a small part of Earth's surface. This shadow then falls on different hemispheres depending on where Earth is in its rotation on its axis. A solar eclipse is a type of eclipse that is only possible to occur during the New Moon phase, when the Moon is positioned directly between the Sun and the Earth. This allows the extraordinary solar eclipse to occur. During a solar eclipse, people satnding on the darkest part of the Moons shadow can see a Total solar eclipse, where the Sun is completely covered and looks like a ring covered in fire. The people who are standing in lighter shadows see a partial eclipse, where only a part of the Sun is blocked. Solar eclipses vastly influence tides because when the Sun, Earth, and the Moon line up, their combined gravitational pull creates stronger spring tides. Spring tides just mean tides that produce higher high tides and lower low tides. You may be wondering, Why don't solar eclipses occur every month? This is because the Moon's orbit is tilted, so its shadow usually passes over or below Earth. Solar eclipses can only occur during special times of the year, which are called 'eclipse seasons'. In this season, the alignment of the Earth, Moon, and Sun becomes perfect enough for an eclipse to occur. Eclipse seasons only happen about 2 times a year, meaning solar eclipses can only happen about twice a year as well. This makes them extremely rare, as they can be in pretty much the entire world from Greenland to Australia, and they only happen twice a year. In simpler terms, a solar eclipse is when the Moon covers the Sun and creates a flaming golden ring around itself (the Moon), which makes an unbelievably cool effect to witness from Earth.

What is the difference between solar and lunar eclipses?
-
The difference between Lunar and Solar eclipses is easily visible to the human eye, but may be hard to explain. During a Solar eclipse, the Moon moves directly between the Sun (on the left) and the Earth (on the right), as you can see in diagram 2. In Lunar eclipses, the Moon is on the very right, the Earth in the middle, and the Sun on the left side, as seen in diagram 1. Solar eclipses can only occur during a new moon, while Lunar eclipses can only occur during a full moon, meaning that they are opposite. During a Solar eclipse, the Moon gains something that looks like a fiery golden ring around it, with an aura that can make you blind. A Solar eclipse may not always be a total Solar eclipse, as it can also be a partial or annular Solar eclipse, which depends on how close the Sun, Moon, and Earth align. A Lunar eclipse turns the Moon into a fiery reddish colour, while it can become darker depending on the alignment of the planets. The key difference between the two types of eclipses is what gets blocked. A solar eclipse blocks the Sun, occurs during the day, and is only visible from limited areas on Earth at a time. A lunar eclipse, on the other hand, blocks sunlight from reaching the Moon, happens at night, and can be seen from large regions on Earth. Both eclipses occur due to the alignment of the Sun, Moon, and the Earth, but the size of Earth's shadow compared to the Moon makes Lunar eclipses far easier to witness.
