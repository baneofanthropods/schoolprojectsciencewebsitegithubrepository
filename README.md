The Earth, Sun, and Moon system
-
The Earth, Sun, and Moon system is a group of 3 celestial bodies held together by gravity, which keeps each body in orbit. Gravity keeps Earth in its revolution around the Sun and keeps the Moon in its revolution around the Earth. Because each body moves at different speeds and angles, in a pattern, we see repeating natural cycles such as Moon phases, tides, seasons, and eclipses. Overall, the Earth, Sun, and Moon system is responsible for many natural cycles that affect life on Earth, including day and night, seasons, tides, moon phases, and eclipses. These cycles occur because of the constant movement of each body and the gravitational forces acting between them.

Movements
-
Earth spins on its axis, completing one revolution every 24 hours, which is called one day. This rotation causes day and night. This is because Earth rotates; when one hemisphere is experiencing night, the other is experiencing day. This is because only one of the hemispheres can be facing towards the sun at a time. Earth also completes one full revolution around the sun every 365 days, creating the seasons. The moon rotates on its axis and completes one full revolution around Earth in about 29.5 days. A rotation is a spin in a place on its axis, while a revolution is a movement around another object. Gravity controls these motions and determines how the Sun, Earth, and Moon align throughout the year.

Effects on Earth
-
Our model demonstrates Solar and Lunar eclipses, which occur when the Earth, Sun, and Moon align in a specific way. A solar eclipse occurs when the Moon moves between Earth and the Sun, casting a shadow that falls onto Earth, while a Lunar eclipse occurs when the Earth moves between the Sun and the Moon, causing Earth's shadow to fall on the Moon. These events depend on the angle of the Moons orbut and which hemisphere is positioned inside the shadow. During these alignments, the combined gravitational pull of the Sun and Moon also strengthens spring tides, producing higher high tides and lower low tides. These effects occur because gravity pulls ocean water into bulges that shift as the Moon moves around Earth.
